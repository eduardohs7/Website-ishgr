About the event.

The International Conference (CHAGS) is one of the most traditional and relevant global events in the fields of Anthropology, Archaeology, and Human Ecology.

Held under the auspices of ISHGR, the 14th edition consolidates itself as a strategic space for debate, training, and the dissemination of scientific knowledge focused on sustainability, bioeconomy, and traditional peoples, with the Amazon as the central stage for contemporary discussions.

The CHAGS conferences bring together researchers, anthropologists, archaeologists, activists, and other members of the international community to discuss hunter-gatherer societies, their histories, ways of life, and contemporary challenges.

About ISHGR.

The International Society for Hunter-Gatherer Research (ISHGR) was established in 2014 to promote research and a better understanding of hunter-gatherer societies, both past and present.

History and Mission

The Society emerged from a decision made during a plenary session at CHAGS 10, held in Liverpool in 2013. It became the first formal international network for scholars specializing in hunter-gatherer studies and took on an important role in supporting the continuity of the CHAGS conferences.

ISHGR strengthens collaboration among researchers and institutions worldwide. Its membership includes approximately 400 individual and institutional members. The Society also supports the journal Hunter Gatherer Research (HGR), an international, multidisciplinary, peer-reviewed publication covering research on hunter-gatherer societies.

CHAGS Milestones.

The Conferences on Hunting and Gathering Societies (CHAGS) trace their origins to the landmark Man the Hunter meeting, held at the University of Chicago in 1966. This gathering represented an important turning point in the development of hunter-gatherer studies and helped establish the foundations for an international academic dialogue on these societies.

What began as an academic meeting gradually developed into a conference series connecting researchers from different countries and disciplines. Over more than five decades, CHAGS has brought together anthropologists, archaeologists, historians, ecologists, activists, Indigenous representatives, and other researchers interested in the diversity of hunter-gatherer societies.

The conferences provide a space for sharing research and discussing different ways of life, social and cultural practices, relationships with the environment, traditional knowledge, and the transformations experienced by these communities throughout history. Their interdisciplinary approach encourages dialogue between academic research and the experiences and perspectives of the peoples who are directly connected to these discussions.

CHAGS is also committed to being an inclusive and accessible international gathering. Its conferences welcome participants from different backgrounds and career stages, encouraging the participation of early-career researchers. Beyond academic presentations, they offer an important space for activism, discussion of the rights and challenges faced by hunter-gatherer peoples, and reflection on their place and future in an increasingly interconnected world.

Following the establishment of the International Society for Hunter-Gatherer Research (ISHGR), the conference series regained a regular schedule, with editions planned every three years. Each meeting introduces new themes and perspectives, reinforcing the importance of international collaboration and the recognition of hunter-gatherer peoples, their knowledge, resilience, and contributions to contemporary discussions.
